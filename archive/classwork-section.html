Classwork Projects

  • What’s the male version of “Karen”?

    Computational Modeling of Linguistics · Terry Regier

    Used 140 years of U.S. Social Security baby-name records to test a viral claim that “Terry” is the male equivalent of “Karen,” by building a model that searches thousands of names for the closest statistical match rather than eyeballing a chart.

  • Why some sounds blur together in the ear

    Computational Modeling of Linguistics · Terry Regier

    Built a Bayesian model of vowel perception and fit it to real listener data to explain why sounds near a “typical” vowel feel more similar to each other than sounds sitting on the boundary between two vowel categories.

  • Do all languages picture the same “best red”?

    Computational Modeling of Linguistics · Terry Regier

    Analyzed color-naming data from 110 languages spoken by non-industrialized societies worldwide to test whether cultures agree on the best example of colors like red or blue, and measured which languages depart most from that global pattern.

  • Can a language’s sounds reveal its neighbors?

    Computational Modeling of Linguistics · Terry Regier

    Trained a classifier on the sound systems of over 350 South American languages to test whether languages near the Andes share distinctive phoneme patterns — a signature of long-term contact between unrelated languages.

  • Can kids learn a grammar rule they’re never taught?

    Computational Modeling of Linguistics · Terry Regier

    Modeled whether a simple statistical learner exposed only to ordinary parent speech could work out an intricate rule about contractions that some linguists argue is too subtle to pick up from experience alone.

  • How a toddler’s language complexity catches up

    Computational Modeling of Linguistics · Terry Regier

    Measured the linguistic complexity of a two-year-old’s speech against his mother’s using information theory, then tracked how that gap narrows as the child grows older.
